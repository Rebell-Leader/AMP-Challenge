METHOD SUMMARY — Training data, external databases, and computational filters

1. OVERVIEW AND DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
Our approach is filter-first rather than generator-heavy. We deliberately use a
simple, fully interpretable generator that samples strictly on the manifold of
real antimicrobial peptides (AMPs), and invest the effort in an independently
validated ranking stack. This choice is motivated by the competition design:
Phase-1 scoring rewards not only diversity/novelty but also surrogate-predicted
activity and distributional similarity to real AMPs, and Phase-2 draws 25
peptides uniformly at random from the top-100 — so every candidate in the top-100
must be strong, not just the best few. A tight, uniformly high-quality list beats
a diverse-but-uneven one.

2. TRAINING DATA AND EXTERNAL DATABASES
Generator training corpus (~40,000 unique peptides):
- MarLys-AMP antibacterial set (the reference set shipped with the competition
  starter kit, data/antibacterial.fasta): 39,448 unique sequences, all filtered
  to the 8–50 aa canonical-amino-acid window.
- DBAASP-derived peptides with measured activity, accessed via the QMAP benchmark
  package (qmap-benchmark), restricted to those with at least one panel-relevant
  strain MIC <= 16 uM (the competition potency threshold).
The union (~40,047 sequences) is the corpus the generator learns from.

Surrogate-model training data:
- QMAP benchmark (DBAASP-sourced), which provides per-strain MIC labels and HC50
  consensus values with a homology-aware train/test split (sequences clustered at
  0.6 identity so near-duplicates never straddle the split). After filtering to
  8–50 aa canonical sequences we used 9,497 labeled peptides (by record), with
  per-strain label counts ranging from ~6,000 (E. coli) to ~300 (E. cloacae).
  MIC units were verified to be micromolar (uM) directly from the QMAP source
  (DBAASPDataset.with_efficiency_below docstring: "threshold (in uM)").

All databases used are open/permissively licensed: MarLys-AMP (CC-0), DBAASP via
QMAP. No proprietary data was used.

3. GENERATOR
An order-3 residue Markov model over the 20 canonical amino acids, trained on the
~40,000-peptide corpus (7,991 order-3 contexts). Sequence length is drawn from the
empirical corpus length distribution and an end-token is honored only at/after the
sampled target length, so generated lengths match the natural AMP distribution
(median 18 aa; the raw library has median length 18, mean 18.8, mean net charge
+2.9 — matching MarLys at 18/18.7/+2.8). The generator is pure Python/NumPy, seeded
(seed 2027), and produces a byte-for-byte reproducible 50,000-sequence library.
No fine-tuning of any large model is used for generation; the competition
explicitly states fine-tuning is not required and single-CPU methods are eligible.

4. SURROGATE SCORING STACK (used for top-100 selection)
- Sequence embeddings: ESM-2 (esm2_t33_650M_UR50D), mean-pooled over residues.
- Per-strain MIC regressors: gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost) on log10(MIC),
  trained per panel species on the ESM-2 embedding plus 26 physicochemical
  features (composition, net charge, hydrophobicity, cationic fraction).
- Calibration: every strain head was evaluated on the QMAP homology-aware test
  split. Only the four heads with Pearson correlation >= 0.5 were retained:
  E. coli (0.54), S. aureus (0.53), K. pneumoniae (0.52), S. enterica (0.64).
  The low-data strains (P. aeruginosa, A. baumannii, enterococci, E. cloacae,
  B. subtilis) did not reach 0.5 and were NOT used to make activity claims.
- Safety: an HC50 regressor (PCC 0.51) used as a soft/directional filter.
- Potency classifier: active if predicted MIC <= 16 uM on any panel strain
  (AUROC 0.74).

5. TOP-100 SELECTION (the "manual intervention"/filter step)
From the 50,000-sequence library we pre-filtered on a fast physicochemical
heuristic (charge, hydrophobic fraction, cationic fraction) to ~5,000 candidates,
scored those with the full ESM-2 + surrogate stack, and selected the top-100 by:
- Predicted potency and predicted-active coverage across the four reliable strains
  (both Gram-negative and Gram-positive).
- Safety (HC50) and internal diversity (a cluster cap so the random Phase-2 draw
  never lands on redundant near-duplicates).
- Hard novelty/compliance gates (Section 6).
The submitted top-100 (broad-spectrum) has 100% predicted-active coverage across
all four reliable strains, median predicted potency 0.98, and median HC50 ~99 uM.

6. COMPLIANCE AND NOVELTY FILTERS (hard gates, all enforced)
- Alphabet: 20 canonical amino acids only.
- Length: 8–50 aa.
- Uniqueness: all 50,000 library sequences unique; all 100 top sequences unique.
- Library novelty: no sequence exactly matching any MarLys-AMP entry.
- Top-100 novelty: every top-100 sequence has <80% identity to any MarLys entry.
  We enforce this with Levenshtein ratio < 0.79 (the shipped verifier uses
  Levenshtein.ratio > 0.8 as the binding gate). Worst-case top-100 identity is 0.77.
- Subset property: every top-100 sequence is a member of the 50,000-library.
- We additionally screened out any sequence whose substring matches common
  secret-key regexes (e.g. AWS-key-like patterns), because such substrings can be
  corrupted in transit by automated secret scanners; this affected a small number
  of sequences that were regenerated.
All of the above were re-verified end-to-end with the organizers' own
verify_submission.py functions on the final submitted files.

7. INDEPENDENT VALIDATION (not used in selection)
To guard against selecting peptides that merely satisfy our own surrogate
(circularity), we scored the top-100 with three activity predictors named in the
competition documentation that had NO role in our selection pipeline:
- AMPredictor (Dong et al., eLife 2025): ESM-1b + GNN + contact map. Validated at
  PCC 0.50 vs QMAP E. coli ground truth.
- MBC-Attention (Yan et al., mSystems 2023): iFeature + Multi-Branch CNN,
  predicts E. coli MIC. Validated at PCC 0.71 — the strongest independent judge.
- Pandi DeepAMP (Pandi et al., Nature Communications 2023): one-hot + Gram-split
  CNN/LSTM.
All three independently score the top-100 as substantially more potent than
typical natural AMPs (83–100% of the list beats the median real AMP under each
judge; the strongest judge, MBC-Attention, predicts median E. coli MIC ~2 uM,
roughly an order of magnitude below the 16 uM potency threshold).

We also tested whether a generative optimization pass could improve the list: a
bounded DeepAMP (Li et al., Nature Communications 2024) seed-and-mutate pass using
MBC-Attention as the acceptance gate produced apparent improvement under
MBC-Attention, but the two other independent judges (AMPredictor, Pandi) showed
the "optimized" variants were in fact worse — a clear overfitting-to-surrogate
signal. We therefore REJECTED the optimization and kept the original top-100. This
is the discipline a single-submission competition demands.

8. COMPUTE AND REPRODUCIBILITY
Everything runs on a single workstation (no GPU required for the core method) at
zero cloud cost. The submission is packaged as a reproducible uv project with a
fixed seed; re-running the generator reproduces the 50,000-library byte-for-byte
(verified: identical SHA-256 across independent runs). Public model weights only
(ESM-2, and the named surrogates for validation). Code, weights, and training-data
provenance are open for co-authorship eligibility.
