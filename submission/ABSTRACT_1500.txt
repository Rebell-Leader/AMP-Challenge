We generate antimicrobial peptides with an order-3 residue Markov model trained on ~40,000 real AMPs (the MarLys-AMP antibacterial set plus DBAASP-derived peptides with measured MIC <= 16 uM). Generation is deliberately simple and on-manifold: every sequence is sampled from the empirical residue statistics of known actives, so the 50,000-peptide library matches the length (median 18 aa) and net-charge (mean +2.9) distributions of natural AMPs by construction. The design philosophy is filter-first, not generator-heavy: quality comes from ranking, not from an elaborate generator. We built a surrogate scoring stack (ESM-2 embeddings + per-strain XGBoost MIC regressors, calibrated on a homology-aware QMAP split, plus an HC50 head), keeping only the four strain heads that reached PCC >= 0.5. Candidates are filtered for predicted potency, safety, novelty (no exact MarLys match; <80% Levenshtein identity to any MarLys entry), and diversity to select the top-100. We validated the top-100 with three independent activity predictors named in the competition (AMPredictor, MBC-Attention, Pandi DeepAMP) that were not used in selection; all agree the list is substantially more potent than typical natural AMPs, with the best-validated judge predicting median E. coli MIC ~2 uM. Everything runs on a single workstation at zero cloud cost.
